Finding an earthquake's epicenter, visually

Guess a spot, predict when each station should have felt the shaking, and walk the guess downhill until the predictions match the recordings.

How we find it: walk the guess downhill

Problem: an earthquake starts at a hidden spot, the epicenter, and its shaking spreads through the ground at about 6 km/s. Each seismograph station records how many seconds after the start it felt the shaking, give or take a little timing noise. Where did the quake start?

Approach: if the quake had started at a guess g, station i would have felt it ‖g − si‖ / v seconds after the start. We score the guess by J, the mean squared gap between those predicted delays and the recorded ones, then step it downhill on J (gradient descent), starting next to the station that felt the shaking first.

J(g) = 1n ∑i (‖g − si‖v − ti)2
∇J(g) = 1n ∑i 2ei g − si‖g − si‖ v g ← g − α ∇J(g)

ei = ‖g − si‖ / v − ti is station i's predicted minus recorded delay, and v = 6 km/s. Each walk starts 1 km off the first station to feel it (in x and in y) and stops once a step moves the guess less than 0.001 km, or after 1,000 steps. We walk once for each step size α = 0.01, 0.02, …, 0.99 and keep the final guess with the smallest J. The map shows the walk with that best α.

How accurate is it? Many quakes at once

Question: one quake can be lucky or unlucky. How far off is our estimate on a typical quake, and when does it go badly wrong?

How to read it: we simulate many quakes, run the method from the first tab on each one, and place one dot per quake at its miss: the distance from our final guess to the true epicenter (km, log scale). Gray dots always use the prompt's default settings (10 stations, 50 km spacing, 0.2 s timing noise). Blue dots use the settings you pick above them.

Try this

  • Press ▶ Run and watch the orange guess walk downhill on the map while J drops in the chart beside it.
  • Click or drag in the J chart to jump to any step.
  • Press ↻ New quake, or drag a setting, to draw a different quake.
  • Drag a setting and compare the blue dots with the gray ones.
  • Hover a dot to preview that quake; click it to pin it, then press Step through it.
  • Press ↻ Resample to draw a fresh set of quakes.
